Cards reach [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) through the
[AnkiConnect](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/2055492159) add-on, so Anki has to be running with
AnkiConnect installed. **Settings → Anki** shows how cards are made with the dictionary you use.

You don't need to change AnkiConnect's `webCorsOriginList` for any of them. Kimochi sends every
AnkiConnect request from the app itself, not from a web page, and AnkiConnect accepts those with
its default settings.

## Who makes the card

That depends on your [dictionary engine](/lookups/dictionaries/). **Settings → Anki** explains it
for the engine you use, with a button to its Anki settings.

| Engine | Who makes the card | Where to set it up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Built-in Yomitan** | Yomitan | **Open Built-in Yomitan Anki settings**: turn on **Enable Anki integration**, then choose the deck, note type and fields. |
| **Hachidori** | Hachidori | **Open Hachidori Anki settings**: its **Templates** set the deck, note type and fields. |
| **10ten** | Nobody: 10ten doesn't make Anki cards. | |
| **Yomitan API** | Kimochi | **Settings → Anki → Mining**, below. |

Built-in Yomitan and Hachidori keep their own Anki settings, the AnkiConnect address and API key
included: set those there, as you would in the browser.

## The AnkiConnect connection

**Settings → Anki → AnkiConnect** shows whether Kimochi can reach AnkiConnect, whatever dictionary
you use: the Anki tab, sentence audio and the Yomitan API pop-up all use this connection. The
defaults are AnkiConnect's own, so it usually works untouched. It checks again every few seconds
while the tab is open; **Check** asks at once. (Built-in Yomitan and Hachidori add their cards
with their own AnkiConnect address, set in their Anki settings under **Mining**.)

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Address** | `http://127.0.0.1:8765` | Where AnkiConnect listens. Change it if you changed AnkiConnect's `webBindPort`; a port number on its own (`8770`) is enough. |
| **API key** | None | Only if AnkiConnect's config sets an `apiKey`. Kept on this computer and never synced. |

| Status | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Connected** | Nothing. It shows the AnkiConnect API version. |
| **Not running** | Open Anki, and check that AnkiConnect is installed. |
| **Wrong port** | Nothing answers at the address, or something that isn't AnkiConnect does. If AnkiConnect answers at the default address instead, a button switches to it. |
| **API key needed** / **Wrong API key** | AnkiConnect has an `apiKey`: enter the same key. |
| **Not answering** | Anki is busy, for example with a dialog open. |

## Kimochi's card builder (Yomitan API)

With the Yomitan API engine, Kimochi's own pop-up builds the cards. Below the connection in
**Settings → Anki → Mining**:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Enable Mining** | Off | Shows the **+** (Add to Anki) button in the lookup pop-up. |
| **Deck** / **Note type** | — | Where cards go, and which note type they use. |
| **Detect from Anki** | — | Finds your Lapis, Kiku or Senren note type and the deck holding most of its notes, as Hachidori's setup does, and fills in its fields. You see what it found before anything changes; **Apply** also turns on mining. |
| **Import from Yomitan** | — | Reads the deck, note type and field mappings from a Yomitan settings export. Its tags join [card enhancement's](#tags). |
| **Field mapping** | — | For each field of the note type: a Yomitan marker, `(sentence)`, or literal text. |

The markers are `expression`, `reading`, `glossary`, `dictionary`, `frequencies`, `pitch-accents`,
`part-of-speech`, `tags`, `conjugation`, `audio`, `sentence` and `sentence-furigana`.

In the pop-up, each entry has a small **+** (Add to Anki) beside its audio button, in the entry's
top corner. It shows only once a deck and note type are chosen; until then **Settings → Anki** says
what's missing. Once the card is in Anki, or when the word is already in your deck, the **+** becomes a
green check: click it to open the card in Anki. If adding fails, it shows a red alert; hover it for
the reason (for example **Anki not running**) and click it to try again.

## The Anki tab

The main window's **Anki** tab (beside **Log** and **Notebook**, or <kbd>⌘3</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl+3</kbd>)
lists the cards you made in this session, newest first, whichever made them: Kimochi's pop-up,
Built-in Yomitan or Hachidori. They stay with the session, so switching sessions shows that
session's cards, and with an account they sync with it. Click a card to open it in Anki's browser.
A green audio mark means Kimochi added its sentence audio (and word audio) after it was made; hover
it to see which. Search finds a card by its word, reading or deck, and the footer counts the
session's cards, today's and those with audio.

Each card's **⋯** menu (or a right-click on the card) has **Open in Anki**, **Add sentence audio** (for a card made without it:
Kimochi puts its line's kept clip in the card's empty audio field) and **Delete…**, which removes it
from the tab and, if you tick **Also delete the card in Anki**, from Anki too. Edit a card in Anki.
The tab needs Anki running: when AnkiConnect doesn't answer, the tab says why, with a button to
**Settings → Anki → AnkiConnect**.

## Sentence audio

Turn on **Enhance Anki cards** in the sidebar (or **Enhance with sentence audio** in **Settings →
Anki → Card enhancement**) and Kimochi records what your computer plays, finds the audio of each
line you read, and adds it to the new cards you make in Anki automatically. It works with Built-in
Yomitan, Hachidori and the Yomitan API pop-up. The cog beside the sidebar switch opens Card
enhancement, and the faint info mark says what it does. It is experimental: a clip may not always
be the right line, and the [Anki tab](#the-anki-tab) shows which cards got audio.

The switch is there whatever your [source](/reading/sources/): a window or screen, Quick Shot, the
clipboard or a WebSocket. With Quick Shot, take the shot soon after the line is said: only the
last **Seconds kept** of audio are held (30 by default), so a later shot finds no audio for its
line. An amber dot on the info mark says so; raise **Seconds kept** under **Advanced** if you need
longer.

Turning it on downloads the speech model (240 MB, run on your computer), which listens for each
line's words to find where it was said. Recording starts once the model is downloaded; the section
shows the download's progress until then. After that it stays quiet unless something is wrong (no
permission, no audio device), and says so. Audio is only kept in memory: nothing is saved but the
clips of the newest lines and the clip in your card.

**Audio field** is the field in your note that gets the line's audio. Kimochi finds it in Anki
when you turn the feature on, and again when the AnkiConnect address or the Yomitan API's note type
changes: Lapis and Kiku call it `SentenceAudio`, Senren `sentenceAudio`. You can type another.

### Advanced

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Start offset** / **End offset** | 0 ms | Milliseconds added to the clip's start or end; negative moves it earlier. |
| **Seconds kept** | 30 | A card gets audio only for a line whose audio is still held (10–120). |
| **Clips kept** | 15 | The newest lines' clips kept on your computer, to play from the Log and add to later cards. |

- **macOS** records system audio under the **Screen Recording** permission, and leaves Kimochi's
  own sounds out. macOS shows its screen-recording indicator while it is on.
- **Windows** leaves Kimochi's own sounds out on Windows 10 (2004) and later.
- **Linux** and the **Steam Deck** need PipeWire, as SteamOS and most desktops have. Kimochi's own
  sounds are recorded too, so keep sound effects off while you mine.

## Tags

Under sentence audio in **Settings → Anki → Card enhancement**, **Add tags** (on by default) tags
every new card you make, whichever dictionary made it: Kimochi's pop-up adds them with the card,
and Built-in Yomitan's and Hachidori's cards get them as soon as they're added (on top of any tags
of their own).

**Tags** is a comma-separated list, `kimochi, {{session-name}}` by default.
`{{session-name}}` becomes the name of the session you're reading, with `_` for its spaces (Anki
tags can't hold spaces), so a card mined in **Chrono Trigger** is tagged `kimochi` and
`Chrono_Trigger`. Below the field, Kimochi shows the tags a card made now would get.
