The first time Kimochi opens, a short welcome guide sets up what it needs. You can open it again from
**Settings → General → Help → Welcome guide**.

<Screenshot id="welcome-intro" alt="The welcome guide's first page: the Kimochi icon, Read and play in the language you're learning, and a Get started button." width={640} />

## The steps

| Step | What it does | Shown on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What language are you learning?** | Picks the [language you're learning](/language/). | All |
| **Allow screen recording** | Lets Kimochi capture the window you read. | macOS |
| **Allow Accessibility** | Optional: lets a click on your source bring that exact window forward. | macOS |
| **Set up GNOME integration** | Adds the small GNOME extension the [overlay](/install/linux/#the-overlay-on-wayland) and pop-ups need there. Log out and back in once afterwards. | Linux, GNOME |
| **How should Kimochi read text?** | Picks the [OCR engine](/reading/ocr-engines/). | All |
| **Choose a pop-up dictionary** | Picks and downloads a [dictionary engine](/lookups/dictionaries/). | All |
| **Set up …** | Sets up that engine (for Yomitan, a dictionary: Jitendex for Japanese). | All |
| **Connect your browser's Yomitan** | Sets up the Yomitan API instead, when you chose it. | All |
| **Add Kimochi to your Steam library** | Adds Kimochi to Steam for Gaming Mode. | Steam Deck, Desktop Mode |
| **You're ready to read** | Recaps the loop and offers a test lookup. | All |

## Language

The guide asks which language you're learning. Japanese is chosen to begin with; scroll or search
the list for another. The OCR and dictionary steps that follow are set up for the language you
pick, and you can change it later in **Settings → Language**. Every language but Japanese is
experimental, and the step says so when you pick one. See [Choosing a language](/language/).

<Screenshot id="welcome-language" alt="The language step: What language are you learning?, a searchable list of languages with Japanese ticked, and Continue." width={640} />

## OCR engine

Cloud engines send each capture online and may need credentials. Google Lens is selected to begin with; see [OCR engines](/reading/ocr-engines/) for
the differences. Only engines that can read your language are listed (see
[OCR engines by language](/language/support/#ocr-engines)).

## Dictionary engine

Kimochi looks words up with a pop-up dictionary engine. Choose one and Kimochi downloads it from its
official release (Yomitan is recommended). You can switch in **Settings → Dictionary**.

<Screenshot id="welcome-engine" alt="The Choose a pop-up dictionary step, with Yomitan selected and a Download 15 MB button." width={640} />

### Set up Yomitan

Yomitan needs at least one dictionary:

- **Install Jitendex** (Japanese only; chosen to start with) downloads the Jitendex
  Japanese–English dictionary and enables it in your current Yomitan profile. It carries on in the
  background if you press **Continue while installing**.
- **Choose your own** (Japanese), then **Open dictionary settings**, opens Yomitan's settings, to
  import dictionaries or a settings backup from Yomitan in your browser.
- **Import a … dictionary** (other languages) does the same: download a dictionary for your
  language from [Yomitan's dictionary list](https://yomitan.wiki/dictionaries/) (the step links
  to it), then import it in Yomitan's settings.
- **Do it later**, then **Continue**, moves on; add a dictionary later in **Settings → Dictionary**.

<Screenshot id="welcome-dictionaries" alt="The Set up Yomitan step: Install Jitendex, Choose your own, and Do it later." width={640} />

Hachidori offers a set of recommended dictionaries the first time its settings open. 10ten works
straight away and downloads its full dictionaries in the background. Both are for Japanese only.

## You're ready to read

The last step recaps the loop: **choose and crop**, **scan**, **look up** (hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> and
hover a word, or use controller reading navigation; press **B** to go back to your game). With
Yomitan, **Test a lookup of …** looks up a sample word in your language (<span lang="ja">日本語</span> for Japanese) to
check that your dictionary answers.
