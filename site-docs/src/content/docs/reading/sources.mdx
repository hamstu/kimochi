The **source** is where the Log's lines come from. It sits at the top of the main window's sidebar:
a live preview of the window or screen, its name, and **Crop area** and **Change source**.

<Screenshot id="source-card" alt="The sidebar's source card for a game window, with its live preview, Crop area and Change source buttons, highlighted." width={240} />

Clicking the card brings the source forward (**Bring to front**, or **Go to full screen** for a
full-screen game). Hover it and the ✕ in the corner clears the source.

## Kinds of source

Open **Change source** (or **Choose a source** when none is set) to switch:

| Source | What it reads |
| --- | --- |
| **Window or screen** | An app window or a whole display, with OCR. |
| **Quick Shot** | No source to choose: the Scan shortcut asks you to draw an area anywhere, then reads it. |
| **Clipboard** | Text copied to the clipboard, for example by a text hooker. |
| **WebSocket** | Lines from a text source on your network. |

**Clear source** forgets the current one, and **Source settings** opens **Settings → Sources**.

## Choosing a window or screen

**Window or screen** opens a grid of **Screens** and **App windows**, each with a live thumbnail.
The current one is badged **Current**. Pick a window (a game, a manga reader, a browser, a video player) to read just that window wherever it
is, or a screen to read everything on it.

<Screenshot id="source-grid" alt="The window and screen picker, with thumbnails of two screens and several app windows." width={720} />

- **macOS** lists windows once Screen Recording is allowed; until then the grid shows
  **Allow Screen Recording…**. See [Install on macOS](/install/macos/#screen-recording).
- **Linux on Wayland** uses your desktop's own dialog instead: **Choose New Source…** opens it,
  **Use Current Source** keeps the last pick. See [Install on Linux](/install/linux/#choosing-what-to-read).
- **Steam Deck Gaming Mode** lists your running games by name, with their Steam icons and live
  previews. See [Gaming Mode](/controller/gaming-mode/).

With **Auto switch sessions by window name** on (the default), each window you read gets its own
[session](/log/sessions/), created the first time.

## Crop area

A crop area limits scans to part of the source, such as a game's dialogue box or a video's
subtitles, so menus, HUDs and other text stay out of the Log.

<Screenshot id="crop-menu" alt="The source card with three numbered marks: 1 the Crop area button, 2 Change OCR crop area in its menu, 3 the crop outlined on the live preview." width={520} />

1. Click **Crop area** under the source's preview.
2. Choose **Set OCR crop area…** (**Change OCR crop area…** once there is one). The region editor
   opens over the source, as a blue box:
   - Drag to draw the box. Hold <kbd>Space</kbd> while drawing to move it.
   - Drag inside the box to move it, or its edges and corners to resize it.
   - Arrow keys nudge it by one point (<kbd>Shift</kbd> + arrows by ten).
   - Press <kbd>Return</kbd>, double-click inside, or choose **Confirm** to keep it; <kbd>Esc</kbd>
     cancels.
   - On a touchscreen, drag with a finger to draw, move or resize it; the handles are bigger there.
   - With a controller (always on a Steam Deck in Gaming Mode), push a stick: the left stick moves
     the box's top-left corner and the right stick its bottom-right, each marked with crosshairs.
     Press **A** to save it or **B** to cancel; the bar at the bottom says which, and you can tap it.
3. The crop shows as a frame on the live preview, and every scan reads only what's inside it.

**Clear OCR crop area**, in the same menu, removes it.

### Anki crop area

The Anki crop area is the part of the source that goes on your Anki cards as the [screenshot](/lookups/anki/#screenshot), such
as the game's scene without its dialogue box. Without one, cards get the whole source.

Choose **Crop area → Set Anki crop area…** (**Change Anki crop area…** once there is one). The same
editor opens, as a green box; **Save** keeps it. It doesn't change what's read, and nothing is
scanned. It shows as a green frame on the live preview. **Clear Anki crop area** removes it.

## Quick Shot

With **Quick Shot** there is no window to choose. Press the Scan shortcut (or **Draw and scan**) and
draw an area anywhere on the screen; Kimochi reads it once. Pressing the Scan shortcut again does
what <kbd>Return</kbd> and <kbd>Esc</kbd> do: it scans the area you drew, or closes Quick Shot if you
haven't drawn one yet. Auto OCR isn't available in Quick Shot.
