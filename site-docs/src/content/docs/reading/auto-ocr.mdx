With **Auto OCR** on, Kimochi watches the source and scans it each time the text on screen changes
and then settles, so new dialogue lands in the Log without pressing anything. Turn it on with the
**Auto OCR** switch in the sidebar, in **Settings → OCR & Overlay → Auto OCR** (the same switch),
or from the [controller menu](/controller/#the-controller-menu).

While it is on, the preview shows an **Auto** badge with a green dot and the Scan button becomes
**Force rescan**. Auto OCR works with a window or screen source; Quick Shot has no Auto OCR.

**Force rescan** (or the scan shortcut) clears the text read so far and scans again at once, with
Auto OCR starting over from what is on screen now. Use it when Auto OCR seems stuck: text that
does not update, or an engine that stopped answering.

## How it decides when to scan

Auto OCR watches the screen with a local OCR engine several times a second (nothing is sent anywhere
for this) and only hands a capture to your OCR engine once the text stops changing: by default, two
identical readings in a row. Text that never stops animating is read anyway after a while, and
requests are spaced out so a fast-moving scene doesn't flood the engine; only the newest frame is
kept.

Which engine watches depends on the one you read with:

- **A local engine** (PaddleOCR, MeikiOCR, Apple Live Text or OneOCR) watches for itself, so what
  the overlay shows between scans always matches what it read.
- **An online engine or Manga OCR** gets the best watcher already on your computer: PaddleOCR on a
  graphics card, then Apple Live Text or OneOCR, then PaddleOCR on the CPU, then MeikiOCR. Nothing
  is downloaded just to watch.
- **With none of those** (for example a fresh Linux install), Kimochi compares pictures instead of
  text, so a moving background can also set it off. Download PaddleOCR in
  [OCR engines](/reading/ocr-engines/#paddleocr) for smarter watching.

**Settings → OCR & Overlay → Auto OCR** names the watcher under the switch. When the watcher is slow
(PaddleOCR on a CPU, say), Auto OCR looks less often, leaving more of the processor to your game.

The Log's activity ticker (bottom of the Log) says what happened: **Screen settled, scanning…**,
**Added 1 line**, **Same text as before, not added**, **No new text**. Click it for the recent history.

If scans fail several times in a row, Auto OCR pauses (**Auto-scan paused: it failed several times
in a row**); manual scans still work.

## Settings

Open **Advanced options** (the arrow beside it) under **Settings → OCR & Overlay → Auto OCR**:

| Setting | Options | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Watch the screen with** | Automatic, any local engine you have, or Image comparison | Automatic | The engine that notices new text before yours reads it. Only shown when you read with an online engine or Manga OCR. |
| **Adaptive polling** | On / off | On | Looks more often while text is moving, and backs off once it settles. |
| **Settle after** | 2, 3 or 4 readings | 2 readings | Identical readings in a row before the text counts as settled. More means fewer requests and a longer wait after a line finishes drawing. |
| **Give up waiting after** | 2, 4 or 8 seconds | 4 seconds | Text that never stops animating is recognised anyway after this long. |
| **Minimum time between requests** | No limit, 0.8, 1.2 or 3 seconds | 1.2 seconds | A floor on how often the OCR engine is called. Lines that arrive during the wait are not queued; only the latest is kept. |
| **Base poll interval** | 250 ms – 5 s | 500 ms | How often to check for screen changes (the starting interval with adaptive polling). |

## Scan after a button press

**Settings → OCR & Overlay → Auto-scan → Scan after a button press** scans automatically a moment
after you press a chosen gamepad button, for games where you advance the text with a button. Set the
**Gamepad trigger** and the **Scan delay** (100 ms to 3 s, default 500 ms).
