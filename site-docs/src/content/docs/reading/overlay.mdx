The overlay draws each scan's text back over the source (a game, a manga page, a video), so you can hover a word where it appears on
screen instead of in the Log.

<Screenshot id="overlay-demo" alt="Kimochi's overlay over a visual novel: each word of the dialogue boxed, the hovered one highlighted, with its dictionary entry above." width={560} />

## Turning it on

Turn on **Show overlay** in the sidebar (under Auto OCR), or **Overlay enabled** in
**Settings → Overlay**. The overlay follows the window you read, and clicks go through it to the game
(see [Clicking through to the game](#clicking-through-to-the-game)).

<Screenshot id="overlay-switch" alt="The sidebar with the Show overlay switch highlighted." width={272} />

## Looking up words

Hover a word with <kbd>Shift</kbd> held to open its pop-up (see
[Which key to hold](/lookups/looking-up/#which-key-to-hold)), or turn on
[Look up on hover](#look-up-on-hover) and just rest the pointer on it. With a controller, open the
[radial menu](/controller/#the-radial-menu) and choose **Scan** or **Read along** to step through the
words with the D-pad. See [Looking up words](/lookups/looking-up/).

### Look up on hover

With **Look up on hover** on (**Settings → Overlay**, or **Settings → Shortcuts & Controls**), resting
the pointer on a word on the overlay looks it up, with no key held. Passing over text on the way
somewhere else doesn't. The pop-up closes once the pointer leaves the line and the pop-up. Clicks still
go through to the game; hold the lookup key to click the text, or to look a word up straight away.
It isn't offered on Linux with Wayland, nor in Steam Deck Gaming Mode.

## Clicking through to the game

Text on the overlay often sits over the game's buttons: a dialogue box you click to advance, a
choice, a menu. So clicks go **through** the overlay's text to the game, unless you hold
<kbd>Shift</kbd>:

- **Without Shift**, clicking on scanned text clicks the game underneath.
- **With Shift held**, text lights up as you point at it, and you can look words up, click a word,
  and move or use the [hooked line's card](/reading/text-hooker/#the-card-over-the-game). Once text
  is lit it stays clickable while your pointer is on it, even after you let go of Shift.
- **With a dictionary pop-up open**, your next click outside it only closes the pop-up and doesn't
  reach the game. The pop-up itself, and the hooked line's card's ⋯ menu once it is open, stay
  clickable without Shift.

To have scanned text take your clicks instead, turn off
**Click through text to the game** in **Settings → Overlay**. You still hold <kbd>Shift</kbd>
to look words up.

To use another key than Shift (some visual novels skip text while Shift is held), click
**Lookup key** in **Settings → Shortcuts & Controls** and press the one you want: Ctrl, Alt (Option
on a Mac) or, on a Mac, Command. Or pick it from the list under the **▾** button next to it. Your
dictionary still looks words up with its own key, Shift by default, so set the same key in its
settings too.

You can also press a **mouse button** there: the **middle button** (the wheel), or a mouse's
**back** or **forward** side button. Hold it and move over the text to look words up, exactly as
with Shift, and Shift goes back to being just Shift for the game. Your dictionary needs no change.
What happens to the click itself:

- **Windows**: a press over the overlay's text, the Text Hooker's card or a dictionary pop-up stays
  with Kimochi, so the game never sees it. Anywhere else the button works as usual (a middle click
  in your browser still opens a link in a new tab).
- **Mac**: the same, once Kimochi has **Accessibility** (**Settings → Sources**). Without it the
  button still looks words up, but the game gets the click too.
- **Linux (X11) and Steam Deck desktop**: the button looks words up, and the game gets the click
  too, so choose one the game doesn't use.
- **Linux with Wayland**: not available. Wayland doesn't let one app see buttons pressed over
  another's window, so the lookup key stays a key there.

If a keyboard shortcut uses the same mouse button, it doesn't run while that button is the lookup
key; Settings points this out.

A game that runs as administrator on Windows (Genshin Impact, for one) hides the keyboard from
Kimochi. When you choose one, Kimochi offers to **Restart as administrator**, so the lookup key works
over it; see [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting/).

On **Linux with Wayland**, this works on **KDE Plasma** and on **Hyprland** (there the key is always
Shift, which Kimochi binds while it runs, see [Linux](/install/linux/)). On other Wayland desktops
Kimochi can't see Shift while another app is in front, so there the overlay's text always takes your
clicks. On KDE Plasma with **Legacy X11 App Support** set to **Never**, and on Hyprland until Kimochi
has seen Shift, hovering a word looks it up, without Shift.

### The hint bar

While there is text on the overlay, a small bar below it says what Shift does. Clicks go through the bar too, except on its **×**, which closes it for good.
**Settings → General → Help → Overlay hints → Show again** brings it back. On Linux with
Wayland, where Shift doesn't gate clicks, the bar isn't shown.

## Options

**Settings → Overlay**:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overlay enabled** | Off | The sidebar's **Show overlay**. |
| **Group text into regions** | Off | Marks nearby lines with one shared box to hover, instead of a box for each line. |
| **Look up on hover** | Off | Rest the pointer on a word to look it up, no key needed. See [above](#look-up-on-hover). |
| **Filter out furigana and small text** | On | Japanese only. Leaves reading aids above kanji out of the overlay and the Log. |
| **Click through text to the game** | On | Clicks on scanned text go to the game unless you hold Shift. See [above](#clicking-through-to-the-game). |
| **Exclude overlay from screen recording** | On | Hides the overlay and dictionary pop-ups from screenshots and screen recordings, so Kimochi never reads its own text back. Turn it off to capture them, for example to share a clip. |
| **Pause the game while reading** | Off | Freezes the game while you read. See [Pausing the game](#pausing-the-game). |
| **Drop lines** | No Japanese | Leaves lines without text in [your language](/language/) out of the overlay and the Log; named for it (**No Korean**). The same setting as in [The Log](/log/the-log/#which-lines-are-kept). |

The same page has the [Text Hooker's card](/reading/text-hooker/#the-card-over-the-game) look (on
Windows and Linux), the [radial menu](/controller/#the-radial-menu) and

## Pausing the game

With **Pause the game while reading** on (**Settings → Overlay**, or **Auto game pause** in the
sidebar, under Show overlay), Kimochi freezes the game while you look up a word, use the radial menu
or draw a crop area, and lets it carry on when you're done. It's off by default and experimental:
online games may disconnect, and Kimochi leaves alone a game that uses anti-cheat, one the system
protects, and one that runs with more rights than Kimochi (as administrator), and tells you when it
can't pause one. If a game ever stays frozen, **Resume paused game → Resume** in Settings starts it again.

## Overlay appearance

**Settings → Overlay → Overlay appearance** changes how the overlay is drawn. A large live preview
at the top, in the language you're learning, follows each setting as you change it; point at a word
in it to see its card:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Highlight colour** | Indigo | The tint and outline over text you can hover; pick a preset or any colour. |
| **Highlight opacity** | 40% | How strongly the highlight shows before you point at it, from **Off** (no highlight) to 100%. |
| **Hover card appearance** | Same as Kimochi | The card hovered text turns into: **Same as Kimochi** (follows the app's appearance), **Light**, **Dark**, **Blend**, which fills each card with the colours behind that word on your screen and picks light or dark text to match, as if the game drew it, or **Transparent** (no card). |
| **Outline the word being looked up** | On | Shown only while the hover cards or the Text Hooker's card are Transparent: a faint outline over the word being looked up, as Read along draws. |
| **Dictionary opacity** | 100% | How solid the dictionary pop-up is, from 10% to 100%, so the game shows through it. Works with every dictionary; the same setting as in **Settings → Dictionary**. |

<Screenshot id="settings-overlay-appearance" alt="Settings, Overlay appearance: the live preview with a word looked up, Highlight colour, Highlight opacity, Hover card appearance and Dictionary opacity." width={760} />

### Transparent

With **Hover card appearance** set to **Transparent**, a word you point at doesn't turn into a card,
even while you hold the lookup key: the game's own text stays in view. Looking words up works exactly
as before (hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> and point at a word, or rest the pointer on it with
[Look up on hover](#look-up-on-hover) on), and the dictionary pop-up opens as usual, with a faint
outline over the word it matched (**Outline the word being looked up**). The text always lets clicks
through to the game while it's Transparent. The highlight is separate and still shows; set
**Highlight opacity** to **Off** as well and nothing at all is drawn over your game, so the dictionary
pop-up (and the outline) is all that appears. Lower **Dictionary opacity** to see the game through
the pop-up too.

The [Text Hooker's card](/reading/text-hooker/#the-card-over-the-game) has its own look, set
separately, so the overlay can be transparent while the card stays visible, or the other way round.

## Platform notes

- **macOS:** Game Mode throttles the overlay while a full-screen game is in front; Kimochi tells you
  when it happens. See [Install on macOS](/install/macos/#game-mode).
- **Linux on Wayland:** the overlay needs the desktop's layer-shell support or, on GNOME 46 to 51,
  the GNOME extension the welcome guide sets up. Without them the switch reads **Overlay unavailable**. See
  [Install on Linux](/install/linux/#the-overlay-on-wayland).
- **Debug overlay** (**Settings → General → Diagnostics**) marks the scanned text boxes over the
  source and opens the Diagnostics window, to check what the OCR found.
